# Agile Team Charter

## Team and Project
- Project: SafeGuard Soshanguve
- Team members:  Nompumelelo Mbatha(Designer), Ryle Peter May(Team Leader),Angel Masonganye(Researcher), Yamkela Mgcubhe(Writer/Editor), Tebogo Makgato 
- Method: Agile (sprints, Trello board, weekly stand-ups and logbook)

## Vision Statement
To make Soshanguve a safer community by giving residents an affordable, locally run security service backed by a mobile alert app for fast emergency response.

## Shared Purpose
Crime is a serious problem in Soshanguve, and many residents cannot afford big private security companies. Our team set out to build SafeGuard, a community-based security solution that uses local guards and a mobile alert system, priced at R100 to R200 a month. We committed to testing our idea with real community members before building the full product.

## Core Values
- Respect: every team member's ideas are heard, even when we disagree.
- Honesty: we say early when we are stuck or behind.
- Accountability: if we commit to a task, we finish it or tell the team in time.
- Collaboration: we help each other instead of working alone.
- Community focus: decisions are made with the people we are building for in mind.

## Working Agreements
- Stand-ups: we hold a stand-up every week. Each person answers three things: what I did, what I will do next, and what is blocking me. These are recorded in our Agile Logbook.
- Communication: WhatsApp for quick daily updates and reminders, Microsoft Teams for meetings and sharing files, and Trello for tracking work.
- Task tracking: all work lives on our Trello board as user stories broken into tasks. Each task has an owner and moves from To Do to In Progress to Done.
- Deadlines: tasks must be finished before the sprint ends. If someone cannot finish, they tell the team early so the work can be shared.
- Decisions: we discuss as a team and agree together before moving on.
- Documentation: every stand-up and sprint is written into the Agile Logbook.

## Boundaries
- Missing a meeting: a team member sends an apology on WhatsApp beforehand and catches up using the logbook and Trello.
- Missing a deadline: the team asks why, offers help, and the task is reassigned if needed.
- Conflict: we talk about the issue, not the person. If the team cannot solve it, we ask our lecturer to help.
- Fairness: everyone contributes to the project and no one carries the whole team.

## Reflection
Writing this charter helped me see that a team is not only about the product. It is also about agreeing on how we work together. By deciding up front that we would use WhatsApp for quick updates, Teams for meetings and files, and Trello for tasks, we avoided the confusion of important information being scattered in different places.

The working agreements were the most useful part for me. The weekly stand-up format kept us focused and made problems show up early, and writing each one into the logbook gave us a record of our progress. Managing our backlog on Trello made it clear who was responsible for what. This became especially important when we prepared our presentation for our lecturer, because we could show exactly what had been done and what was still in progress.

Our value of community focus became real after our lecturer's feedback. We had assumed the community would pay for SafeGuard, and the feedback pushed us to test that assumption instead. We set up the Instagram page @safeguard.soshanguve, shared screens of the app, asked people to comment YES, and set a target of 50 followers to measure real interest. We also narrowed our pricing to R100 to R200 instead of guessing. Hearing from real people reminded us that we were building for the community and not only for an assignment, and it shaped what we prioritised.

What I would improve is the boundaries. We agreed on them, but I would make them more specific next time, for example by setting a clear response time in the WhatsApp group and a set step to follow when someone falls behind. I also learned that a charter is a living document. It should be reviewed after each sprint, not written once and forgotten.

This process improved my teamwork, communication and organisation skills, and it prepared me for working in an Agile team in industry.
